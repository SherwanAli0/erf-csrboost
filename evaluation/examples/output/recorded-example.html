About this example

Synthetic loan outcomes, 1,500 rows, five models

A seeded synthetic table of 1,500 loan-style rows with a rare outcome (default, 196 rows). Five models were compared on one stratified split with the settings the app uses for an uploaded file. The simplest model, logistic regression, has the highest average precision here (0.492); the best of the other four is 0.475.

Data: Generated by evaluation/examples/make_recorded_example.py from a seeded random number generator (seed 6). It contains no real person, no external data and nothing confidential.

Reading note: The signal in this table is additive by construction, which suits a linear model, so this example says nothing about which model suits other data. The test partition has 39 positive rows; small differences between models should not be read as a ranking. Costs are illustrative assumptions in arbitrary units, not measured savings.

Cost assumptions

  "formula": "FP * false_positive_cost + FN * false_negative_cost + flagged * review_cost"

Costs are assumptions entered by the person who ran the analysis, in one unit they chose. They turn counts of false alarms, missed positives and reviews into a number; they are assumptions, not measured savings, and nothing here shows what a real deployment would save or cost.

Metric definitions

Average precision
Precision at each score cutoff, weighted by the recall gained at that cutoff (scikit-learn, non-interpolated). Higher is better. It depends on how common the positive class is, so compare models on the same rows only.
ROC AUC
The probability that a random positive row scores above a random negative row. 0.5 is chance; it measures ranking only and ignores any threshold.
Precision, recall
Precision is true positives divided by rows flagged. Recall is true positives divided by all positive rows. A zero denominator is reported as unavailable.
Brier score, log loss
Mean squared error and logarithmic loss of the predicted probability. Lower is better. They are shown only for probability scores and do not by themselves show calibration.
Threshold policy
A row is flagged when its score is at or above the threshold.
Review budget policy
The k highest scores are flagged. Ties are broken by input row order, and a tie at the cutoff is reported with possible minimum and maximum positives found.

  • Saved predictions cannot establish that model training, splitting or preprocessing avoided leakage.
  • The evaluation role is user-declared. Score orientation and row alignment must be supplied correctly.
  • Inspecting many policies on a test set is exploratory; choose a final policy using independent validation data.
  • Metrics are descriptive point estimates, not confidence intervals or evidence of general superiority.
  • Calibration bins are descriptive. A probability score is not automatically a calibrated event probability.
  • Average precision uses recall-increment weighting, not trapezoidal PR area; prevalence affects its interpretation.
  • Cost estimates use hypothetical additive assumptions and do not establish realized savings or review outcomes.
  • JSON and HTML contain aggregates, not raw labels or prediction rows; aggregates can still be sensitive.
